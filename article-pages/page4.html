NVIDIA RTX 5090 Review: Is the 600W Power Draw Worth It?

Alex Chen

Senior Hardware Analyst • Jan 15, 2026

12 min read

We benchmark the new Blackwell architecture. It destroys 4K ray tracing, but your PSU might need a serious upgrade.

The King is dead, long live the King. After two years of dominance by the RTX 4090, NVIDIA has finally unleashed the GeForce RTX 5090. Based on the new Blackwell architecture, this card promises a generational leap that makes the previous flagship look like a mid-range toy. But with a massive 600W TDP, the question isn't just "can you afford the card?"—it's "can your house handle it?"

1. Specs: The Numbers Game

NVIDIA hasn't held back. We are looking at a 32GB GDDR7 memory buffer with bandwidth exceeding 1.5 TB/s. Here is how it stacks up against its predecessor:

Spec RTX 4090 RTX 5090
Architecture Ada Lovelace Blackwell
VRAM 24GB GDDR6X 32GB GDDR7
TDP (Power) 450W 600W
CUDA Cores 16,384 21,760

2. The 600W Elephant in the Room

Let's address the controversy immediately. Yes, the TDP is rated at 600 Watts. In our stress testing using Cyberpunk 2077 with Path Tracing overdrive, we saw peak system power draw hit 850W (CPU included).

This means if you are running an 850W power supply, you are living on the edge. NVIDIA has introduced the new 12V-2x6 connector standard to prevent the melting issues seen on the 4090, but the sheer thermal output is undeniable. You will need a high-airflow case, and ideally, a 1000W or 1200W ATX 3.1 certified PSU.

"Warning: Do not attempt to daisy-chain PCIe cables for this card. Use a native ATX 3.1 cable to ensure stable power delivery."

3. Performance: Is it worth the heat?

In short: Absolutely.

At 4K resolution, the RTX 5090 is roughly 60-70% faster than the RTX 4090 in pure rasterization. Turn on Ray Tracing and DLSS 4.0, and that gap widens. We were hitting 120 FPS in Alan Wake 2 at 4K Max Settings. It is the first card that truly makes 8K gaming a viable conversation, not just a marketing gimmick.

The Verdict

The NVIDIA RTX 5090 is an engineering marvel that demands respect—and a robust electrical circuit. It is not for the budget-conscious, nor for those with small form-factor cases. But if you want the absolute best performance money can buy, Blackwell has set a new bar that likely won't be cleared for years.

Just make sure you budget an extra $200 for a new Power Supply Unit.
